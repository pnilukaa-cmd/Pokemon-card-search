# Mega Chandelure ex: Telepathic Energy and Hilda

Reviewed 2026-10-27 from a submitted list: 3 Mega Chandelure ex on a
3-2 Litwick / Lampent line with 2 Rare Candy, alongside 2 Mew ex, a
Phantump / Trevenant line with Uxie, 2 Elgyem, Unown and Patrat. It ran
10 Energy (4 Telepathic Psychic Energy), with Enhanced Hammer, Handheld
Fan, Tool Scrapper, Prime Catcher, Mystery Garden and Morty's Conviction.
The submitted Switch SVI 194 and Rare Candy SVI 191 are rotated printings;
this pool carries MEG 130 and MEG 125.

**This list: 72.82% against the 98-deck field at 1000 games per opponent**
(median 77.5%, 89 of 98 winning), on the engine as of aa450cf. On the same
seeds it is **+37.17 ± 1.31** over the submitted list (35.65%), better on
97 of 98. It is also +23.26 ± 1.04 over the field's other Mega Chandelure
ex list (`mega_chandelure_ex_retreat_tax`, 200 games a matchup).

**The changes:**
- **The Chandelure line to 4-4-4, with 4 Rare Candy** (+1 Litwick,
  +2 Lampent, +1 Mega Chandelure ex, +2 Rare Candy).
- **18 Energy, not 10** (+7 Basic Psychic Energy, Legacy Energy).
- **+4 Hilda.**
- **Out:**
  - 2 Mew ex, 2 Phantump, 2 Trevenant, Uxie;
  - 2 Elgyem, Unown, Patrat;
  - 2 Handheld Fan, 2 Enhanced Hammer, Tool Scrapper, Prime Catcher;
  - 1 Mystery Garden.

## The strategy, card by card

**Mega Chandelure ex** (PBL 38, Stage 2 from Lampent, 350 HP, Retreat 2,
weak to Darkness):
- **Binding Flame:** your opponent's Active Pokémon's Retreat Cost is one
  Colorless more. It has no "doesn't stack" clause and works from the
  Bench, so every Mega Chandelure ex in play adds one more.
- **Phantom Maze** (Psychic, Psychic): 130, plus 50 for each Colorless in
  their Active's Retreat Cost. Against a Retreat 1 Active: 230 with one
  Mega Chandelure ex in play, 280 with two, 330 with three.

**Getting there:**
- **Litwick** (PBL 36) is the deck's only Basic.
- **Lampent** (PBL 37): Spreading Light (Psychic) benches up to 3 Lampent
  from the deck.
- **Rare Candy** skips Lampent, from a Litwick that has been in play since
  your last turn.
- **Hilda** finds an Evolution Pokémon and an Energy card: a Lampent or
  Mega Chandelure ex and the Energy to go with it.
- **Telepathic Psychic Energy** (POR 88): attached from hand to a Psychic
  Pokémon, it also benches up to 2 Basic Psychic Pokémon (Litwick).
- **Mystery Garden:** discard an Energy card from hand, draw until your
  hand matches the Psychic Pokémon you have in play.
- **Morty's Conviction:** discard a card, draw one for each of their
  Benched Pokémon.
- **Legacy Energy** (ACE SPEC): one Energy of every type. The first
  Pokémon it's attached to that is Knocked Out gives up one Prize fewer,
  so a Mega Chandelure ex gives up 2.

**What it adds up to:**
- **The submitted list was starved of Energy and of its attacker.** With 10
  Energy it had 1.78 Energy in play at the end of turn 4, and Mega
  Chandelure ex was in play by turn 4 in 39% of games. This list: 2.88
  Energy, and 83% (`energy_curve.py`, 10 games against each field deck).
  Every change that added Energy measured up, about 2.5 points per card
  until the count reached 17. Part of that is this simulator's AI, which
  runs short of Energy where a player wouldn't. Meta decks measure better
  here with 3 Energy in place of their 3 Boss's Orders (+4.59 to +10.15)
  or their Crushing Hammers (+5.45), and no player plays them that way.
  How many of the 18 a player needs isn't measured; the submitted list ran
  10.
- **Mew ex costs this deck points.** It can use Phantom Maze from a Benched
  Mega Chandelure ex for the same damage. But in play it rarely had two
  Energy for it, and its 160 HP gives up 2 Prizes. Under the lookahead
  pilot (50 games a matchup) the list without Mew was still +2.51 ± 0.82;
  greedy said +4.24. So it isn't only the AI misusing Mew. One Mew ex is
  close to free (−0.22 and −0.66) if you want it for the matchups it wins.
- **Trevenant and Uxie lost too.** Painful Memories plus Overwhelming Pain
  is a real combo, but it took 5 cards and two attacking turns, and the
  slots measured better as Lampent, Hilda and Energy.
- **4 Basics measured better than 6 or more,** even with the mulligans:
  the engine plays them, and the opponent's extra draws. Every extra Basic
  lost. A 4-Basic deck still mulligans **60.1%** of the time; Marshadow
  (+0.30 ± 0.25, a 5th Basic) is the measured-neutral way to bring that to
  52.5%.

## How to play it

1. **Turn 1.**
   - Litwick Active; Buddy-Buddy Poffin, Telepathic Psychic Energy (on a
     Litwick) or Ultra Ball for more Litwick.
   - No evolving on either player's first turn.
2. **Turn 2.**
   - Evolve a Litwick into Lampent, then Spreading Light for up to 3 more
     Lampent.
   - Or Rare Candy a Litwick that was in play last turn straight into Mega
     Chandelure ex. Not one put down this turn.
   - Hilda for the piece and an Energy.
3. **Turn 3 and after.**
   - Two Energy on the Active Mega Chandelure ex, Phantom Maze.
   - Keep evolving Lampent on the Bench: every extra Mega Chandelure ex is
     +50 to Phantom Maze.
   - Legacy Energy on the first Mega Chandelure ex likely to be Knocked Out.
   - Boss's Orders on a high-Retreat Benched Pokémon, which Binding Flame
     makes higher: a bigger Phantom Maze, and one it can't retreat from.

**The opponent's outs:**
- **Darkness.** Mega Chandelure ex, Litwick and Lampent are all weak to it.
  The worst matchups include Mega Absol ex (31.8%), Munkidori ex /
  Pecharunt ex (34.2%) and Mega Gengar ex (34.9%).
- **Tauros** (Risky Ruins): 18.2%.
- **Ability lock** turns Binding Flame off: Phantom Maze falls to 130 plus
  50 per printed Retreat Cost.
- **A Mega Chandelure ex gives up 3 Prizes,** 2 with Legacy Energy once.
- **Gusting a Benched Litwick or Lampent** before it evolves.
- **4 Basics:** a 60% mulligan.

## What the simulator found

Each fix has a regression test that fails on the commit before.
1. **"Discard another card" was free** (aa450cf). Morty's Conviction,
   Canari and Iris's Fighting Spirit say "You can use this card only if you
   discard another card from your hand." The cost had no number, so it
   didn't compile and all three cost nothing. Morty's "draw a card for each
   of your opponent's Benched Pokémon" also drew one. Ten field decks run
   one of the three.
2. **The setup baseline ignored this deck's engine** (3dbc366):
   Telepathic Psychic Energy's Bench search, Mystery Garden and Wondrous
   Patch. The submitted list read Mega Chandelure ex in play by turn 6 in
   24.5% of trials without them and 30.2% with them.
3. **The retreat-cost tag missed Talonflame's Aero Chase** ("if the
   Retreat Cost of your opponent's Active Pokémon is 2 or more, +110").
   Fixed in `analyze_mechanics.py`; both mechanic audits still read 0.
4. **Boss's Orders was played for nothing** (243a582) **and never
   stranded** (27be9e3), above. **A gust with no Active to switch with
   crashed** (9d7eb51).

## Cards searched

- **Retreat Cost payoffs** (`damage_scales_with_retreat_cost`, with a raw
  "retreat cost" sweep): Phantom Maze, Marshadow's and Grimmsnarl's
  Shadowy Knot, Ariados's String Bind, Iron Bundle, Throh, Talonflame.
  The sweep's other hits lower your own Retreat Cost (Air Balloon, Rescue
  Board, N's Castle) or punish a 0 Retreat Cost (Carnivine).
- **Retreat Cost raisers** (`retreat_cost_increase_opponent`): Binding
  Flame, Gravity Gemstone (−1.33), Ariados's Big Net (−3.68), Rillaboom's
  Drum Beating.
- **Basic Psychic Pokémon for Telepathic Energy:** Mew ex, Uxie, Unown,
  Elgyem, Phantump, Dedenne, Team Rocket's Chingling, Marshadow, all
  measured above.

**Anti-synergies:**
- Gravity Gemstone raises your own Active's Retreat Cost too.
- Mystery Garden draws to the number of Psychic Pokémon you have in play,
  so it draws little before the board is up.
- Ability lock stops Binding Flame on every copy at once.
