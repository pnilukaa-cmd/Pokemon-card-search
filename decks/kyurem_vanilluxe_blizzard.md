# Kyurem ex / N's Vanilluxe — Blizzard

A competitive shell for `N's Vanilluxe`, after four that were not.

**51.4% mean / 50.0% median / 16-33**, and 52.5% / 48.5% / 15-33 on an
independent seed. The first Vanilluxe build in this folder measured
**17.5%**.

## Vanilluxe earns its slots here — and not for the reason you would guess

Same test that condemned it twice before: build the deck, then build the
identical deck with the entire Vanilluxe line cut, and run both paired.

| | mean | median | winning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **with the Vanilluxe line** | **46.4%** | 44.5% | **11/33** |
| without it | 40.5% | 39.0% | 7/33 |
| *independent seed* | **45.5%** vs 40.3% | | **11** vs 6 |

**+5.9 and +5.2 on two seeds.** That is a real contribution, and it is the
opposite of what the same test said in a `Zoroark` shell and a
`Palossand` shell, where removing Vanilluxe changed nothing.

**But it is not earning them with `Snow Coating`.** Across 20 traced games
the doubler does not appear in the deck's seven most-used attacks at all.
What Vanilluxe is actually doing is **`Blizzard`**:

> `W``C``C` — **120 damage, and 10 to each of your opponent's Benched
> Pokémon.**

A 150 HP Stage 2 that hits for 120 and chips the whole Bench is simply a
good attack, and it happens to share a type and an Energy line with
Kyurem ex. `Snow Coating` is a finisher you press on the turn the board is
already loaded, not an engine. Building around the doubling is what
produced the 17.5%.

## The two things that actually moved the number

Neither was a card choice. Both were counts.

| | mean | change |
| --- | --- | --- |
| first Water build, 12 Energy | 33.1% | — |
| **15 Energy** | 46.4% | **+13.3** |
| **+ 3 `Misty's Vitality`** | **51.4%** | **+5.0** |
| + a 4th Misty's, 12 Basics | 51.3% | flat — plateau |

**Energy was the whole problem.** The trace made it unarguable: at 12
Energy, `Kyurem ex`'s filler `Slash` (`C``C`, 50 damage) was used **38
times** against 7 `Blizzard Burst`. The deck's real attacks both cost
three Energy and it could not pay for either, so it spent the game poking
for 50.

`Misty's Vitality` PBL 111 — *search your deck for up to 4 Basic Water
Energy and attach them to 1 of your Pokémon* — loads a whole attacker in
one card. **Its cost is that your turn ends**, which is exactly the right
price in a deck whose problem was never tempo, it was fuel.

**`Articuno` JTG 161 was tried and cut.** Its Frigid Fluttering fetches
two Water Energy but attaches them *to this Pokémon*, so it accelerates
only itself and then competes for the Active Spot with the cards you
actually want attacking.

## Why Kyurem ex is the partner

`Kyurem ex` BLK 157 — **Basic**, 230 HP, Water.

> `W``W``C` **Blizzard Burst 130**, and 10 damage to each of their Benched
> Pokémon **for each Prize card your opponent has taken.**

A Basic ex needs no evolution line, which matters enormously next to a
Stage 2. And its spread **scales as you fall behind** — at three Prizes
taken it is 130 plus 30 to every Benched Pokémon. That is the deck's
comeback button, and it stacks with Blizzard's chip into a Bench that
Snow Coating can then double.

`Hero's Cape` (+100 HP → **330**) is the ACE SPEC, on Kyurem.

## Sequencing

- **Turn 1-2:** Vanillite and Dunsparce down (`Buddy-Buddy Poffin` reaches
  both — 60 HP and 70 HP). Dudunsparce's Run Away Draw is the draw engine.
- **Turn 2-3:** `Misty's Vitality` dumps four Water onto Kyurem ex. Your
  turn ends — that is the cost, and it is why the card is at 3, not 4.
- **Turn 3+:** Blizzard Burst 130 plus Bench spread, every turn.
- **`Rare Candy` takes Vanillite straight to Vanilluxe**, skipping
  Vanillish — which is why Vanillish is a single copy and only there for
  the games where Rare Candy is prized.
- **Snow Coating** when their Bench is already carrying chip from two
  Blizzards. Doubling 40 across five Benched Pokémon is 200 damage spread
  in one attack for two Colorless.

## Numbers

60 cards, no card over 4, one ACE SPEC, mulligan **22.2%** (11 Basics —
raising it to 12 was tested and measured flat, so the slot stayed with the
Energy line).

1000-opening curve: Vanillite 89.5% by T2, Kyurem ex 63.9% by T2 and
85.7% by T6, **N's Vanilluxe 24.0% by T3 and 50.6% by T6** — Rare Candy
roughly doubles its arrival rate versus the un-Candied builds.

Full field, 33 decks, 200 games each — mean **51.4%**, median **50.0%**,
**16/33**. Independent seed: 52.5% / 48.5% / 15-33.

Best: Feraligatr 82.0%, T.R. Wobbuffet 76.5%, Salazzle ex 74.0%,
Crabominable 69.5%. Worst: Panic Poison 30.0%, Steven's Carbink 34.5%,
the Kangaskhan flip deck 34.5%.

## Counter engines tested as Snow Coating partners

Asked directly whether `Hide 'n' Sneak` pairs with Snow Coating, and
whether any **Ability** can place counters from the Bench. Both were
searched properly and the promising ones were measured.

**There are 22 Abilities in Standard that place damage counters.** These
are the ones that work from the Bench without spending your attack:

| Ability | | |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **`Dusknoir` PRE 37** — Cursed Blast | **13 counters (130 damage)** on 1 of their Pokémon, then this Pokémon is Knocked Out | The biggest single placement in the format. Doubled: **260** |
| `Dusclops` PRE 36 — Cursed Blast | 5 counters, same self-KO | The Stage 1 version |
| **`Froslass` TWM 53** — Freezing Shroud | During Checkup, **1 counter on each Pokémon that has an Ability**, both sides, except Froslass | Passive, free, board-wide, from the Bench |
| `T.R. Ampharos` DRI 74 — Darkest Impulse | 4 counters whenever *they* evolve | Reactive |
| `T.R. Crobat ex` DRI 122 — Biting Spree | counters on 2 of their Pokémon when you evolve into it | One-shot |
| `T.R. Dugtrio` ASC 101 — Holes | 2 counters whenever their Active moves to the Bench | Pairs with your own `Boss's Orders` |
| `T.R. Tyranitar` DRI 96 — Sand Stream | 2 counters on each of their **Basics** each Checkup | Must be Active, so it is not a Bench engine |

### Froslass — right shape, wrong rate

`Froslass` is Water, so it costs no second Energy type, and the deck's
key Pokémon — Vanilluxe, Kyurem ex, Vanillite — **have no Abilities**, so
Freezing Shroud does not touch them. It looked ideal.

| | mean | median | winning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| this deck | **51.4%** | 51.2% | **17/34** |
| + 4 Snorunt / 2 Froslass | 48.6% | 45.2% | 12/34 |

**−2.8 points.** One counter per turn, only on Pokémon that happen to
have Abilities, is not worth six slots. Note it also forced `Dudunsparce`
out of the deck — Run Away Draw is an Ability, so the draw engine would
have been damaging itself every Checkup.

### Hide 'n' Sneak — the maths is real and the deck is not

`Sinistcha` PBL 6's `Matcha Spin` places **4 counters on each of their
Pokémon**; Snow Coating doubles that to **8 counters — 80 damage on every
one of their Pokémon**, about **480 across a full board from two
attacks.** That is a genuine combo on paper.

**Measured: 22.5% mean, 1/34 winning.**

Matcha Spin is gated on **6 Hide 'n' Sneak Pokémon in your discard
pile**, which needs the entire twelve-card fuel package *and* the
discard outlets to get them there — and then a Stage 2 Water line on top.
Two complete engines do not fit in one 60, and the Grass/Water split
makes it worse.

**The better version of the same idea, if you want to build it:**
`Sinistcha` **TWM 22** — a different card from PBL 6 — has
`Cursed Drop` for a single `Grass`: *"put 4 damage counters on your
opponent's Pokémon in any way you like."* **Same four counters, no fuel
gate at all**, and you choose where they land. That is strictly the
better Snow Coating partner, at the cost of going Grass instead of Water.

### The pattern, now measured three times

Every attempt to bolt a second counter engine onto this deck has lost:
Froslass **−2.8**, Hide 'n' Sneak **−28.9**, and in an earlier shell the
Vanilluxe line itself was worth **0.0** next to Palossand ex. A second
package costs six to eight slots, and this deck does not have six to
eight slots spare — it is already paying for a Stage 2 line and fifteen
Energy.

## The honest ceiling

This is a middle-of-the-field deck, not a top one — the Scovillain ex
build in this folder measures 56%. The limit is structural: **both real
attacks cost three Energy in a format where the good decks attack on turn
two**, and `Misty's Vitality` buys the fuel by giving up a turn. Vanilluxe
is a genuine contributor here, but it is contributing a 120-damage
Bench-chipping attack, not the exponential the card seems to promise.
